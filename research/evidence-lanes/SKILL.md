# Evidence lanes

## Why lanes

A reader weighs a number by where it came from. Your own measurement, a company's own claim and a vendor's model are different kinds of evidence, and one sentence that mixes them borrows the credibility of the strongest for the weakest. Labelling each number with its lane, and never blending lanes in one sentence, lets the reader weigh each one correctly and lets you defend each one separately.

## The five lanes

| # | Lane | What it is | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Custom research | Brad's own measurement of public AI answers, on the Wayfinder research engine | Naming rates, citation classes, claim accuracy from a frozen prompt set |
| 2 | Client public sources | The client's own pages, posts, captions and support articles, read by hand | A launch post, a product page, a help article, session captions |
| 3 | Client-reported | What the client says about itself in results and investor settings | Earnings releases, investor sessions, shareholder letters |
| 4 | Third-party tool | Vendor estimates and modelled figures | Modelled prompt volumes, CPC, visibility scores |
| 5 | Published research | Studies and frameworks published by others | Industry benchmarks, academic studies, practitioner KPI sets |

## Rules

1. **One lane per number.** Every number carries exactly one lane label where it appears, or in a source line the reader can tie it to.
2. **Never blend lanes in one sentence.** If a point needs two lanes, use two sentences, each labelled.
3. **Third-party tool numbers are never multiplied into a payoff.** A vendor's modelled volume times your measured rate produces a number that looks measured and is not. Use vendor figures for scale and direction only.
4. **Client-reported figures stay at the scope the client gave.** A product-line figure is not a product figure. A correlation the client reports is labelled as a correlation.
5. **A fact an agent reported but nobody read does not reach a deliverable.** If an automated step says a page states something, a person reads the page before the claim is used. This rule exists because a support article once reached a slide on an agent's "reported, not read" tag.
6. **Every client-facing number is recomputed from stored data** by code separate from the collector, or labelled "not yet recomputed." Code that collected the data shares its bugs; recomputing with separate code is what catches them.
7. **Retired numbers are listed and never reintroduced.** Keep a retired-numbers list with each case. When a figure is withdrawn (wrong denominator, superseded definition, unverified source), it goes on the list with the reason, and every later draft is checked against the list.

## How to apply

Before a deliverable goes out:

1. List every number in it.
2. Tag each with its lane and its source row in the case's numbers ledger.
3. Check each sentence for more than one lane.
4. Check every lane-4 number is not multiplied by anything.
5. Check every number against the retired list.
6. Mark each number recomputed or not yet recomputed.

## What to record

The numbers ledger for each case lives with the case data, not here. Each row carries: the figure, numerator and denominator, surface, dates, lane, source, recomputed (yes, no, date), and status (live or retired, with reason).
